You’ve done the hard part: setting up Moodle, structuring your LMS, and recording your course. But now comes the real question: **[How do you actually sell a Moodle course?](https://www.theeduassist.com/blog/how-to-sell-online-courses-that-actually-convert/)**

Not marketing. Not ads.

But the technical systems, pricing models, and tools that turn your Moodle LMS into a revenue-generating platform.

This guide breaks down:

- Moodle-compatible selling tools & plugins
- Pricing models for course creators
- Cost realities in 2026
- Why many Moodle setups fail to monetize
- A structured system to sell your course effectively

## **The Real Problem: Moodle Is Not Built to Sell (By Default)**

Moodle is a powerful [**LMS (Learning Management System)**,](https://www.theeduassist.com/blog/best-learning-management-systems/) but it is not inherently a sales platform.

That means:

- No built-in checkout system
- No native subscription billing
- Limited payment gateway integrations
- No funnel or product-based selling logic

Research in digital learning environments shows that while Moodle excels in content delivery and structured learning, it lacks strong commercial capabilities unless extended with external systems.  

**In simple terms:** Moodle helps you teach. It doesn’t help you sell, unless you extend it.

## **Step 1: Choose the Right Monetization Model**

**1. One-Time Purchase Model**

- Fixed price (e.g., $49–$299)
- Lifetime access
- Best for recorded courses

**2. Subscription Model**

- Monthly or yearly billing
- Great for academies or ongoing content

**3. Cohort-Based Pricing**

- Fixed enrollment window
- Premium pricing ($200–$2000+)

**4. Certification-Based Pricing**

- Pay for exam + certificate
- Works well for corporate training and L&D programs

## **Step 2: Moodle Plugins & Tools to Sell Courses**

**Core Moodle Payment Plugins**

***PayPal Enrollment Plugin***

- Built-in in many Moodle setups
- Allows users to pay before enrolling
- **Limitation:** Basic UX, no funnel control

***Stripe Integration (via plugins)***

- Modern checkout experience
- Supports subscriptions
- **Best for:** creators scaling globally

***WooCommerce + Moodle Integration***

- [Use WordPress + WooCommerce as a frontend store](https://www.theeduassist.com/blog/lms-implementation-guide/)
- Moodle as backend LMS
- *This is one of the most scalable setups in 2026.*

***Edwiser Bridge (Popular Solution)***

- Connects WordPress + Moodle
- Sell courses like products
- **Features:**
- Course bundles
- Discount codes
- Memberships

***Moodle E-commerce Plugins (like Enrolment Plugins)***

- Custom enrollment-based pricing
- Flexible access control

## **Cost Reality Check (2026)**

**Initial Setup Costs**

- Moodle Hosting: **$20–$200/month**
- Domain & Hosting (WordPress): **$50–$150/year**
- Premium Plugins: **$100–$500/year**

**Development Costs (Optional)**

- Moodle Developer: **$500–$5000**
- Custom plugin integration: **$300–$2000**

**Ongoing Costs**

- Payment gateway fees (2–5%)
- Maintenance & updates

### **Why Most Moodle Course Sellers Fail**

1. Relying only on Moodle (no optimized checkout)
2. Poor pricing strategy
3. No structured content packaging
4. Lack of learner engagement system

### **FAQs**

**1. How can I sell my Moodle course online without marketing tools?**

Yes, you can sell your Moodle course by using built-in or third-party payment plugins like PayPal or Stripe. However, Moodle alone has limited sales functionality. For a smoother buying experience and automated enrollments, most creators use integrations like WooCommerce.

**2. What is the best plugin or software to sell courses on Moodle in 2026?**

**Edwiser Bridge with WooCommerce** is one of the most effective solutions.

**3. Is Moodle a good platform for selling online courses or just for teaching?**

Excellent for course delivery and learner management, but needs additional tools for selling.

**4. What is the ideal pricing strategy for selling a Moodle course in 2026?**

Typically **$49 to $500+**, depending on depth, niche, and value offered. Subscription and bundle models are also popular.

**5. Do I need WordPress or WooCommerce to sell Moodle courses successfully?**

Not mandatory, but highly recommended for a professional storefront, better payments, and higher conversions.
