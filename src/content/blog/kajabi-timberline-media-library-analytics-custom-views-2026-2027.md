When managing a high-volume online academy, simply having all your videos in one folder is not enough. You need to know how those videos are performing. Does a 4-minute lesson on landing page optimization hold student attention better than an 18-minute masterclass? When a promotional trailer is embedded on an open marketing page, what percentage of viewers watch past the two-minute mark before clicking the purchase button?

In earlier iterations of online learning platforms, video analytics were blended together into a single generic play count. 

With the launch of **Timberline Media Library Enhancements**, Kajabi equips creators with per-placement video telemetry and customizable asset views. 

Throughout 2026 and upcoming 2027, this intelligence allows educational teams to iterate on course content with data-backed precision.

## The Power of Per-Placement Telemetry

A video asset rarely exists in only one context. A creator might embed a lesson clip inside three distinct areas: an open blog article, an opt-in lead magnet page, and an advanced module inside a flagship academy.

```
                  [Master Video Asset in Library]
               "Framework Demonstration (8:30 Mins)"
                                 │
         ┌───────────────────────┼───────────────────────┐
         ▼                       ▼                       ▼
  [Placement A: Blog]     [Placement B: Funnel]   [Placement C: Member Lesson]
  - 1,200 Page Views      - 450 Qualified Leads   - 88 Enrolled Students
  - 14% Play Rate         - 68% Play Rate         - 94% Play Rate
  - Avg Watch: 1:45 Mins  - Avg Watch: 5:10 Mins  - Avg Watch: 8:12 Mins
  - High Churn at 2:00    - Call-to-Action Click: - 91% Full Completion
                          - 28% to Checkout
```

Viewing separate analytics for each placement prevents inaccurate conclusions. A video that struggles to convert cold blog readers might be performing exceptionally well among dedicated enrolled students who appreciate deep technical nuance.

## Custom Media Views: Organizing for Agency Scale

For agencies and multi-instructor academies managing hundreds of files, browsing an endless chronological list is inefficient. Timberline introduces **Custom Views**:

### 1. The Production Pipeline View
Filtered to display video files uploaded in the past 14 days whose status is marked as `Draft` or `Pending Captions`. Video editors use this view to ensure all raw uploads are reviewed before publishing.

### 2. The High-Engagement Asset View
Sorted by highest average watch percentage. Instructional designers review these top-performing assets to identify presentation techniques that resonate with the audience.

### 3. The Re-Shoot Audit View
Filtered for lessons with completion rates below 50%. This flags confusing or slow-paced lessons that require re-recording or breaking down into shorter chapters.

## Practical Deployment: Setting Up Custom Views and Inspecting Telemetry

To set up your operational views and analyze media performance in Kajabi:

### Step 1: Open Media Analytics
From your administrative sidebar, navigate to **Media > Analytics**.

### Step 2: Build a Filtered View
Click **New View**. Select your sorting parameters:
* Filter by: `Asset Type: Video`
* Placement filter: `Product: Masterclass 2026`
* Sort order: `Average Engagement Rate (Ascending)`
* Save this view as `Needs Revision - Low Engagement`.

### Step 3: Inspect Video Drop-Off Curves
Select any individual video asset to open its engagement telemetry graph. Identify where sharp downward dips occur along the timeline:
* A drop at 0:30 often indicates a rambling introduction.
* A drop at 4:15 might signal a sudden confusing shift in technical vocabulary.
* A steady horizontal line indicates compelling, well-paced instruction.

## Frontline Case Study: Refining Course Modules to Double Completion Rates

In mid-2026, [TheEduAssist](/kajabi-services/) conducted a performance review for an enterprise technical coding bootcamp hosted on Kajabi:

### The Starting Situation
The bootcamp featured 48 video lectures. Student completion rates hovered at 43%, with multiple students submitting support tickets complaining that the mid-tier curriculum felt overwhelming.

### The Diagnostic Process
1. We utilized **Timberline Per-Placement Analytics** to inspect engagement curves across every lesson.
2. In Module 4, Lesson 2 (a 24-minute lecture on relational database architecture), we noticed an 82% viewer drop-off occurring at precisely 7 minutes and 40 seconds.
3. Reviewing that exact timestamp revealed that the instructor had switched from clear visual diagrams to 15 minutes of unannotated terminal command code without visual aids.

### The Educational Iteration
* We cut the 24-minute video into three focused 7-minute lessons.
* We added visual callout graphics and clickable video chapters to highlight core command syntax.
* We deployed the revised clips via the library global swap feature.

### The Measured Results
* **Module 4 Completion Rate:** Rose from 18% to 76% in the subsequent student cohort.
* **Overall Academy Graduation:** Climbed from 43% to 68%.
* **Support Inquiries:** Technical help tickets related to that database lesson dropped by 72%.

## Best Practices for Video Telemetry Hygiene

To derive actionable insights from your media dashboard:

* **Wait for Meaningful Sample Sizes:** Do not overhaul a video after only three students have watched it. Allow at least 25 to 50 completions before making major curriculum adjustments.
* **Evaluate Context Before Editing:** A low play rate on a supplementary resource is normal; focus your attention on required core syllabus videos.
* **Maintain Clean File Titles:** Name your assets descriptively so custom views remain easy for editors and assistants to navigate.
