Order bumps have long been one of the most effective tools for increasing average order value (AOV) in digital marketing. Adding an irresistible, low-friction add-on right before the purchase button typically yields acceptance rates between 15% and 30%. However, standard static order bumps carry a severe structural defect: every buyer sees the exact same offer, regardless of their purchase history.

When a loyal student who previously spent \$500 on your core program visits your checkout to buy a new coaching package, showing them a \$37 bump for a workbook they already own is a poor user experience. It erodes brand credibility, causes checkout hesitation, and frequently leads to accidental duplicate orders that require manual refund processing.

With the arrival of **Conditional Order Bumps** in the Kajabi Dispatch roadmap, creators in 2026 and upcoming 2027 can deliver intelligent, context-aware checkout experiences that adapt in real time to the specific buyer.

## The Core Concept: Static vs Conditional Order Bumps

Understanding the difference between legacy order bumps and conditional bumps reveals why checkout personalization is essential for mature educational businesses:

```
[Legacy Static Checkout Flow]
Visitor lands on Checkout ──> Fixed Order Bump Displayed (Regardless of Prior Purchases)
   ├── New Lead: May accept the bump ($37)
   └── Existing Customer: Sees duplicate product -> Confusion, distrust, or accidental re-purchase

[Modern Conditional Checkout Flow]
Visitor lands on Checkout ──> Kajabi Evaluates Customer Profile & Ownership State
   ├── Owns Product A? ──> Suppress Bump A; Dynamically Display Advanced Masterclass ($77)
   └── New Contact?    ──> Display Foundation Starter Templates ($37)
```

By filtering out irrelevant offers and presenting the next logical purchase, you protect your customer relationships while substantially raising revenue per transaction.

## Technical Configuration: Setting Up Conditional Rules in Kajabi

Configuring conditional order bumps requires pairing your checkout offer settings with granular ownership and tag criteria. Follow these tactical steps:

### 1. Select the Base Offer and Add-On Offer
Navigate to **Sales > Offers** and select your primary offer. Open the **Order Bump** section. Instead of selecting a single static product, click **Add Conditional Rule**.

### 2. Define Product Exclusion Criteria
The foundational condition for any professional course portal is **Ownership Suppression**:
* **Condition:** `Customer does NOT own [Product Name]`
* **Action:** Display Order Bump
* **Fallback Behavior:** If condition fails, hide the bump completely or switch to an alternate secondary offer.

### 3. Establish Tag-Based Personalization
For advanced marketing funnels, tailor the bump based on customer tags:
* If contact has tag `VIP Client`, present an exclusive 1:1 strategy session audit add-on.
* If contact has tag `Webinar Attendee`, present the extended slide deck and replay vault add-on.

### 4. Configure Multiple Conditional Alternatives
In the 2026 platform architecture, you can chain conditional rules in priority order. The checkout engine evaluates rules top-down, displaying the highest-priority offer the customer qualifies for.

## Strategic Advice: 4 Rules for High-Converting Conditional Bumps

When planning your order bump strategy, adhere to these proven design guidelines:

1. **Keep the Price Under 30% of the Base Product:** If your main offer is \$200, your order bump should fall between \$27 and \$57. When the bump price exceeds one-third of the core purchase, it triggers deliberate financial evaluation instead of an impulse click.
2. **Focus on Speed, Implementation, or Convenience:** The highest-converting bumps are not more lecture videos; they are shortcuts. Think audio editions, editable Canva/Notion templates, checklists, prompt libraries, and code repositories.
3. **Write Clear, Single-Sentence Benefit Copy:** You have very little visual space on a mobile checkout screen. Ensure the headline states the exact transformation within 10 words.
4. **Test With Incognito and Logged-In Accounts:** Always verify your conditional rules by checking out from an account that owns the product, an account that owns nothing, and an anonymous incognito browser session.
