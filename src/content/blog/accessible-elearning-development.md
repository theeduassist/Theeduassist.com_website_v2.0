## Quick Answer

Building accessible online courses means designing content that works for everyone, including learners with visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive disabilities. TheEduAssist targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA and utilizes inclusive instructional design, proper heading structures, meaningful alt text, and accurate media captions.

Accessibility is not just adding captions at the end of a project. It should be considered during course planning, instructional design, content development, multimedia production, assessment design, LMS implementation and quality assurance.

Accessible learning improves participation for learners with:

- visual disabilities
- hearing disabilities
- limited mobility
- cognitive and learning disabilities
- temporary impairments
- different devices, environments and internet conditions

## Our Primary Accessibility Target

TheEduAssist designs and reviews digital learning content against **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**.

The four WCAG principles are:

- **Perceivable:** Information and user interface components must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive.
- **Operable:** User interface components and navigation must be operable.
- **Understandable:** Information and the operation of user interface must be understandable.
- **Robust:** Content must be robust enough that it can be interpreted by a wide variety of user agents, including assistive technologies.

We design and remediate digital learning experiences toward WCAG 2.2 Level AA and any additional accessibility requirements agreed for the client’s organization, audience and jurisdiction.

## Standards and Regulations We Consider

- **WCAG 2.2 Level AA:** The main technical accessibility target used for the design and evaluation of web-based learning experiences.
- **Section 508:** Relevant to information and communication technology developed, procured, maintained or used by United States federal agencies. Educational and training materials can fall within its scope.
- **ADA:** Accessibility obligations in the United States can depend on the organization and service. The ADA Title II web accessibility rule specifically applies to United States state and local government entities and uses WCAG 2.1 Level AA as its technical standard.
- **EN 301 549:** This European accessibility standard covers accessibility requirements for ICT products and services, including websites, software, documents and other digital technologies.
- **European Accessibility Act:** Applies to specified products and services in the European Union and may be relevant depending on the client’s product, service, market and jurisdiction.

*Applicable legal and procurement requirements should always be confirmed for each organization, contract and jurisdiction.*

## How We Improve Course Accessibility

- accurate captions for video
- transcripts for audio and video
- audio descriptions when essential visual information is not otherwise available
- meaningful image alt text
- accessible charts and diagrams
- logical headings and content structure
- correct reading order
- descriptive link text
- keyboard-accessible navigation and visible focus
- readable typography and suitable colour contrast
- accessible assessments and feedback

## Our Accessibility Process

1. **Discovery and requirements:** Identify learners, platforms, content types, procurement expectations and the agreed accessibility target.
2. **Accessibility audit:** Review the existing course, media, documents, navigation, interactions and assessments.
3. **Remediation or accessible development:** Correct issues or build accessibility into the course from the beginning.
4. **Quality assurance testing:** Test with automated tools, keyboard-only navigation and representative assistive-technology checks.
5. **Reporting and handover:** Provide findings, completed corrections, known limitations and recommendations for future updates.

## Why Accessibility Should Start During Course Design

Correcting accessibility at the end can require re-recording videos, rebuilding interactions, rewriting assessments and remediating documents.

Including accessibility during instructional design can reduce rework, produce more consistent experiences and make future course updates easier.
