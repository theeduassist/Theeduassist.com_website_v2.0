**Preparing Global Teams for New Technology: Lessons from LEAP 2026**

When international companies buy new software or upgrade their digital systems, they often focus entirely on the tools themselves. However, the success of any digital change also depends on the people using it.

As global businesses and technology leaders gather in Riyadh for LEAP 2026, the conversation extends beyond the software being introduced. It also raises an important question: **How should companies prepare their employees to use new technology effectively?**

This is especially important for companies with offices in different countries. Traditional training guidelines can be difficult to roll out consistently across international teams. Practical training that considers employees' roles, locations, and learning needs can make technology adoption easier to manage.

## **The Challenge of Training International Teams**

When a large company introduces a new software tool globally, it may be tempting to send the same English instruction manual to every office. However, this approach does not always work well for international teams.

Distributed workforces can face several training challenges:

- **Different Regional Rules:** Data privacy regulations and digital policies can vary from country to country.
- **Cultural Differences:** The way teams communicate, report issues, or interact with automated systems can differ across regions.
- **Language Barriers:** Text-heavy and complex manuals can make it harder for non-native English speakers to learn quickly, increasing the possibility of mistakes.

To address these challenges, companies can move beyond lengthy training documents and use shorter, easier-to-understand digital lessons focused on practical workplace tasks.

## **Bridging the Gap Between Technology Adoption and Workforce Readiness**

Introducing new technology is only part of the process. Preparing employees to use it effectively is equally important.

As regional and global enterprises implement software, custom platforms, and AI tools, training needs to go beyond general explanations. Employees need guidance that connects the technology to their everyday responsibilities.

### **Start with What Employees Actually Need**

Before introducing a learning program, companies can identify the specific skills employees need to develop.

For instance, when a company introduces a new customer database, employees may not need a broad explanation of the software's history. They may need clear guidance on creating accounts, updating records, managing information, and following company procedures.

This task-focused approach can make training more relevant to the employee's actual role.

### **Keep Training Practical, Short, and Modular**

Understanding a new tool does not always require lengthy training sessions. Organizations can break information into smaller learning experiences that employees can complete alongside their regular work.

Useful approaches include:

- **Short Modules:** Five-minute guides and microlearning lessons can make information easier to absorb.
- **Workplace Scenarios:** Hands-on activities based on realistic tasks can help employees understand how the technology applies to their roles.
- **Global Adaptability:** Well-structured modules can be adapted for different offices, roles, and regional requirements.

Microlearning and just-in-time training are also discussed as emerging approaches to workplace learning and skills development. (++[TheEduAssist](https://theeduassist.com/blog/ai-insights-powering-skills-based-learning-and-professional-growth-in-2026/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)++)

Introducing AI tools requires more than simply giving employees access to them. Training can help employees understand how to use AI responsibly within their roles.

Important areas can include:

- **Verification and Security:** Employees should understand the importance of checking AI-generated information and protecting sensitive company data.
- **Practical Application:** Training can show employees where AI may support everyday tasks.
- **Human Oversight:** Employees should understand when human judgement is still required.
- **Responsible Use:** Organizations can establish clear expectations around privacy, accuracy, and appropriate AI use.

## **A Simple Process for Better Training**

Organizations can use a straightforward process when developing training for a new technology or workflow:

1. **Identify the change:** Clarify the software, technology, or workflow being introduced.
2. **Determine target teams:** Group employees by role and identify their specific learning requirements.
3. **Define required skills:** Establish the practical tasks employees should be able to perform.
4. **Build practical lessons:** Create modules around realistic workplace activities.
5. **Evaluate comprehension:** Use short activities, knowledge checks, or assessments to measure understanding.
6. **Refine continuously:** Use employee feedback to improve unclear or ineffective sections.

This process can help connect training directly to the technology and tasks employees are expected to use.

## **Why Global Training Needs a Flexible Approach**

A training program designed for one office may not work exactly the same way in another country.

International organizations may need to consider:

- Language and communication preferences
- Local regulations and policies
- Different job responsibilities
- Cultural expectations
- Time zones and working schedules
- Different levels of technical experience

This does not mean every office needs a completely different training program. Instead, organizations can build a common core and adapt selected examples, instructions, and activities for different teams.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **How do you train employees when a company operates in multiple countries?**

A company can create a common core training program and then adapt selected content for different regions, languages, roles, and local requirements.

### **What is the best way to train employees on new software?**

Training should focus on the tasks employees actually need to perform. Short demonstrations, practical scenarios, guided activities, and knowledge checks can make software training easier to apply.

### **How can companies overcome language barriers in employee training?**

Organizations can use clear language, localized examples, translated or captioned content where appropriate, and visual demonstrations instead of relying entirely on long written manuals.

### **Why is employee training important when introducing AI?**

AI tools can change workflows and introduce new responsibilities. Employees may need training on appropriate use, verification, privacy, security, and when human judgement should be applied.

### **What is microlearning in employee training?**

Microlearning delivers information through short, focused learning activities. It can be useful when employees need to learn specific tasks without completing lengthy training sessions.

### **How can instructional design help global companies?**

Instructional design can organize complex information into structured learning experiences with clear objectives, activities, assessments, and practical examples.
