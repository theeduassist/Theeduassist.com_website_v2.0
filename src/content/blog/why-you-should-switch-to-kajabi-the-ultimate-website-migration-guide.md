Moving your website to Kajabi sounds exciting until SEO becomes a concern. Many course creators and training businesses worry about losing rankings, broken pages, traffic drops, or learners landing on error pages after migration.That concern is valid.

A website migration affects your pages, URLs, search visibility, and user experience. If migration is rushed, it can impact both traffic and enrollments. When you do things the right way, the **Kajabi website migration** can be easy and your SEO performance will stay the same.

For people who make designs, create courses, teach and work with learning and development teams it is really simple: you want to move your learning platform without losing people’s attention or making it hard for learners to find their way.

At TheEduAssist we see that creators usually focus on making their website look good and moving their content first. They often forget about SEO.

A better approach is to plan both together from day one.


## **Why SEO Matters During Kajabi Website Migration**

When you switch from WordPress, Teachable, Thinkific or some other learning management system platform to Kajabi  The search engines will see your Kajabi pages differently

That can affect:

* existing keyword rankings
* The traffic that comes from search engines
* How pages are indexed
* backlinks
* How learners navigate
* How visible the blog is
* The enrollment pages
A small URL change can lead to broken search results if redirects are missed.

A strong migration plan protects both SEO and learner experience.


## **Common SEO Risks During Kajabi Migration**

Here are common issues that teams face when they are working on websites.

* These issues include old URLs that return 404 pages.
* Sometimes teams also have to deal with missing redirects.
* Blog pages can just disappear.
* Teams also have to fix loading pages and deal with duplicate content.
* When teams migrate their website they often find that metadata has been removed during the migration.
* Image alt text is often missing.

You have to think about what can go during the platform migration and fix these problems before they happen.

## **Step-by-Step Kajabi Website Migration Without Losing SEO**


### **1. Audit Existing Website Pages**

Before you move your website you should take a look at the following things.

* The top ranking blog posts are really important.
* You should also check the landing pages and the course pages.
* Then there are the traffic-driving keywords that bring people to your website.
* Backlinks from websites are also crucial.
* Do not forget to review the metadata, for each page.
This will help you understand what you have on your website


### **2. Map Old URLs to New Kajabi URLs**

This step is really important.

Create a spreadsheet, like this:

Old URL → New Kajabi URL

For example:

/online-course-design-guide → /course-design-guide

This helps prevent broken pages and keeps your rankings safe.


### **3. Set Up Redirects**

When you set up redirects it helps guide people and Google to the new page.

Without redirects a few things can go wrong.

* Users will get errors when they try to visit the page.
* The rankings of your website may drop in search results.
* The traffic to your website can just disappear.
The redirects that [Kajabi](https://kajabi.com)  offers really help to keep everything running smoothly for people who visit your website.


### **4. Move SEO Elements Carefully**

When you are moving things over you need to make sure you keep things the same.

* Page titles should be clear. They should be close to the original version. This helps search engines understand the page.
* You should always check the meta description before you publish something. Make sure your meta descriptions are accurate and tell people what your page is about.
* Your H1, H2 and H3 headings need to be in order. This way people can easily understand what your content is about.
* You should also pay attention to the image alt text. Always check the descriptions of your images to make sure they are good for accessibility and your SEO.
* You need to make sure that the links within your blog still work correctly after you move everything around.
* you should take a look at the blog formatting. Review the headings, check the spacing, also look at the images and tables to make sure they are okay.
Do not publish anything until you have looked over everything.


### **5. Check Mobile Experience**

A lot of people who are learning things use their phones to get to the courses.

Test:

* Navigation is important.
* The first page you see, also called the landing page, should be easy to use.
* When you have to fill out forms it should not be too hard.
* The page where you pay for something, which is called the checkout page should be simple.
* It is also important that the blog is easy to read.
A migration should feel simple for learners on every device.


### **6. Recheck Analytics and Search Console**

After launch you need to monitor a few things:

* You have to check the indexing to see how it is doing.
* There are also traffic trends that you need to be looked at.
* You have to see how many clicks you are getting.
* you also need to find out if there are any broken links that you have to fix.
* Then there are the keyword positions, which tell us how we are doing with our keywords.
The thing is it takes some time to get a picture of SEO results but, over time you will be able to see what is working and what is not.
