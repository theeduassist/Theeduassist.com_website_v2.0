## Quick Answer

To study more effectively, rely on proven cognitive science techniques: spaced repetition, active recall, the Pomodoro Technique, interleaving subjects, and teaching concepts to others. These methods prioritize active engagement over passive re-reading.

Studying hard isn't the same as studying smart. Research in cognitive science has uncovered techniques that dramatically improve how we learn and retain information. Here are five methods every student should know.

## 1. Spaced Repetition

Instead of cramming, review material at increasing intervals — after 1 day, then 3 days, then a week. This forces your brain to actively recall information, strengthening memory each time.

## 2. Active Recall

Close your notes and try to recall what you just studied from memory. Testing yourself is far more effective than re-reading. Use flashcards or simply write down everything you remember.

## 3. The Pomodoro Technique

Study in focused 25-minute blocks, then take a 5-minute break. After four rounds, take a longer break. This keeps your focus sharp and prevents burnout.

## 4. Interleaving

Instead of studying one subject for hours, mix topics in a single session. Jumping between subjects feels harder but leads to deeper understanding and better problem-solving skills.

## 5. Teach What You Learn

Explaining a concept to someone else — or even to yourself out loud — reveals gaps in your understanding instantly. If you can teach it simply, you truly know it.

## Final Thought

The best study technique is the one you'll actually use consistently. Start with one method from this list, practice it for a week, and notice the difference.
